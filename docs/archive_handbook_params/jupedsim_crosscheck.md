# Cross-check against JuPedSim

Generated by `python -m evac.jupedsim_check` (JuPedSim 1.4.2). Our network simulator is compared with three published movement models implemented in JuPedSim, an established open-source pedestrian simulator from Forschungszentrum Juelich, on the same geometry with the same people. Free walking speed 1.25 m/s +-15% everywhere; the JuPedSim models use their default parameters. Times are means over 3 seeds (over the runs that finished).

Models: **CFSM** collision-free speed model (Tordeux, Chraibi & Seyfried 2016), JuPedSim's standard model; **AVM** anticipation velocity model (Xu, Chraibi & Seyfried 2021); **SFM** social force model (Helbing & Molnar 1995).

## What it shows

- Against CFSM, JuPedSim's standard model, our time is within 15% in 4 of 9 situations (median difference +16%, range -14% to +46%).
- Where two or more JuPedSim models finished, ours lies inside their range in 4 of 5 situations: the established models disagree with each other by tens of percent, so no single number is 'the truth'.
- On the real campus floor: 150 people 105.8 s vs CFSM 110.3 s (-4%); 300 people 124.2 s vs CFSM 143.6 s (-14%).
- Door flow: our crowded doors pass fewer people per second than laboratory crowds (Seyfried et al. 2009), like the JuPedSim models do with default parameters. Our flow lies between AVM and CFSM for narrow doors and close to CFSM for wide ones.
- Single rooms (C1, C2): ours is slower than CFSM (+10% to +46%). Our graphs draw the walk from the room centre to the door as a strip as narrow as the door, so people crowd and slow down earlier than in a 2-D room. For these cases our times are conservative.
- Campus floor: ours is about as fast for 150 people and faster for 300 (-14%). Probably streams merging at corridor junctions cost more time in 2-D than our first-come-first-served queues predict, so for large crowds on one floor our times may be optimistic by that margin.
- AVM and SFM often locked up permanently at the 1 m doors and in the floor geometry (default parameters); this is reported as 'jammed', not dropped. CFSM finished almost every run.

### Correction to the earlier door-flow validation

`docs/validation.md` compared the rate at which people are *let onto* a door edge (1.3 persons/s per metre) with laboratory flows. Measured as the rate at which people actually *leave* through a crowded 1 m door, our simulator passes 0.83 persons/s, because walking speed on the door edge falls with density. That is about half the laboratory value and inside the JuPedSim range, so our evacuation times are, if anything, conservative (too slow). Drawn as its own 1 m-long edge, the same door passes only 0.61 persons/s (last person out 171.5 s): door throughput depends on how a door is drawn in the graph, which is a limitation of network models. Our building graphs draw a door as part of the room-to-corridor edge, as in C1.

## Method

- **C1 / C2**: the same rooms, doors and corridors drawn in 2-D for JuPedSim and as a small graph for ours, drawn the way our building graphs are (one edge from the room node through the door, with the door's width). Everyone starts at t = 0. C1: 10 x 6 m room. C2: 12 x 12 m room, 1 m door, 20 m x 2 m corridor, 1.8 m exit door.
- **C3**: the campus ground-floor graph turned into 2-D geometry: each edge becomes a strip of its width (corridor 2 m, room door 1 m, exit door 1.8 m) and each room a pocket behind its door (`figures/fig26b_jupedsim_geometry.png`). Both simulators get the same people: origin room, start time (pre-movement U(0, 30) s), personal speed and exit (shortest path). In JuPedSim people walk the geometric shortest way to that exit. Stairs and upper floors are not part of this check (JuPedSim is 2-D); stair speed is validated against NIST drill data in `docs/validation.md`.
- JuPedSim time step 0.01 s (SFM 0.005 s), ours 0.5 s. A run stops when nobody has got out for 120 s.
